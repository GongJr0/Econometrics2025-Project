\section{Remarks}\label{sec:remarks}

\subsection{On Variable Selection}\label{subsec:remark_var_selection}
The design matrix intentionally omits two main effects that correspond to the interaction terms used in our hypotheses: the level unemployment gap ($u - u^*$) and inflation measured as ($\Delta\log(P^{GDP})$).
We instead include $\Delta (u - u^*)$, incorporating $u - u^*$ and $\Delta\log(P^{GDP})$ only through interaction terms.

\vspace{\baselineskip}
As a robustness check, we estimated an alternative specification that includes the two main effects. (On top of the shock adjusted model)
The main-effect coefficients were found to be insignificant, and both interaction terms lost their significance as well.
The coefficients of interest only showed marginal changes while the $R^2$ had a small increase from 0.45 to 0.47.
However, these additions introduced severe multicollinearity issues, with $cond(X) = 698.2$ and $max(VIF(X)) = 24.72$.

\vspace{\baselineskip}
Therefore, we elected to use the specification with $\Delta\log(P^{GDP})$ omitted as a main-effect, and $u - u^*$ differenced in level form.
We confirmed that the models were qualitatively very similar, while the selected model had tangible benefits in terms of multicollinearity and the preservation of degrees of freedom.
We acknowledge that excluding main effects makes interaction coefficients best viewed as reduced-form conditional relationships rather than clean structural cross-effects.
