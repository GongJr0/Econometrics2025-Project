\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Although our initial design matrix indicated misspecification issues, we were able to address these through shock adjustments and the identification of a structural break.
Ultimately, our final model included every regressor in the initial specification while clearing the diagnostic tests.
Therefore, we believe our current specification is sufficient to clear our first hypothesis. (existence of an efficient model including the two interactions)

\vspace{\baselineskip}

Our hypothesis regarding the insignificance of $okun$ was confirmed. 
Evident by the lack of significance in both $okun$ and $ugap$, we believe that with respect to output growth, the Okun-based influence is fully linear and captured by a single main effect ($ogap$).
Further supporting this view is the sign, magnitude, and significance of the $ogap$ coefficient.
Being the third most influential regression coefficient by percent influence, $ogap$ captures a decently-strong negative relationship, aligning with the underlying theory.

\vspace{\baselineskip}

Our second hypothesis regarding the significance and sign of $philips$ was also confirmed.
Again, $philips$ was one of the most influential regressors, being second only to $pce$.
While the entirety of its influence cannot be attributed to the cross-effect alone\footnote{See Remark~\ref{subsec:remark_var_selection}}, it is clear that inflationary dynamics are important determinants of output growth.

\vspace{\baselineskip}

Overall, we believe that our model was able to explain a decent portion of U.S.\@ output growth dynamics for a prediction horizon of 1 quarter.
Moreover, significant coefficients emerged both from the accounting identity (demand-side) and from the gaps.
Although further robustness checks and extensions are necessary to solidify our claims, we believe that our findings provide useful insights into the lagged determinants of U.S.\@ output growth.
